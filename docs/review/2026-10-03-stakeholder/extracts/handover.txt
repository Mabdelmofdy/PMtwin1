[p1|Title] PM-Twin — Project Master Context & Handover
[p2|Normal] For ChatGPT Business / Team Handover
[p3|Normal] Purpose
This document consolidates the PM-Twin product/domain context, matching rules, recent implementation work, UAT status, customer issues, business decisions, and immediate next steps. It is intended as a master context for a new ChatGPT Business workspace/team.
[p4|Heading 1] 1. Project Overview
[p5|List Bullet] PM-Twin is a Saudi construction collaboration marketplace, conceptually similar to an “Upwork for contracting.”
[p6|List Bullet] The platform connects construction-sector participants through Need/Offer opportunities and supports structured collaboration, matching, negotiation, commercial agreements, and contracts.
[p7|List Bullet] Core objects: Opportunity, Need, Offer, Match, Negotiation, Commercial Agreement, Contract.
[p8|List Bullet] Current work is focused on matching correctness, publishing, commercial structures, company behavior, and post-match deal workflows.
[p9|List Bullet] UAT/demo storage prefixes include PMTWIN_DEMO_ and PMTWIN_UAT_; production is intended for the future API.
[p10|Heading 1] 2. Wizard and Domain Model
[p11|List Bullet] Wizard: Opportunity → Collaboration → Scope & Work → Commercial Structure → Review & Publish.
[p12|List Bullet] Matching topologies: one_way, two_way, consortium, circular.
[p13|List Bullet] Collaboration models: cash_subcontracting, service_exchange, joint_venture, resource_sharing, hiring.
[p14|List Bullet] Exchange modes: Cash, Barter, Equity, Profit Sharing, Hybrid.
[p15|List Bullet] Revenue Sharing is a commercial component and can derive Hybrid; it is not a sixth exchange mode.
[p16|List Bullet] Marketplace is primarily published-only; post-match deal stages include negotiation, commercial agreement, and contract.
[p17|Heading 1] 3. Matching Engine — Current Rules
[p18|List Bullet] Eligible matching is based on published opportunities and excludes self-matches.
[p19|List Bullet] Target role compatibility, required skills/services, budget overlap, timeline compatibility, and model/submodel compatibility are part of matching.
[p20|List Bullet] Model/submodel mismatch is a hard incompatibility.
[p21|List Bullet] Category is a soft compatibility factor, not an exact mandatory match.
[p22|List Bullet] Title and description are informational and are not hard matching criteria.
[p23|List Bullet] Nationwide Saudi coverage is treated as 100% location coverage for Saudi locations.
[p24|List Bullet] Boundary-date timeline overlap remains compatible.
[p25|List Bullet] Missing/incomplete timeline information scores 0 for Timeline Fit.
[p26|List Bullet] Overall threshold is 50%.
[p27|Heading 1] 4. Matching Score Weights
[p28|List Bullet] Skill Match 25%; Exchange 20%; Value 20%; Budget 10%; Timeline 10%; Location 10%; Reputation 5%.
[p29|List Bullet] Category compatibility was added as a small bonus on top of the existing weighted score without redistributing those weights, capped at 1.
[p30|Heading 1] 5. Timeline Fit
[p31|List Bullet] Timeline Fit now uses inclusive calendar-day overlap against the Need window.
[p32|List Bullet] Formula: Timeline Fit = clamp(overlapDays / needDays, 0, 1).
[p33|List Bullet] Missing/incomplete timelines score 0; disjoint timelines score 0; boundary overlap remains eligible and positive.
[p34|List Bullet] Timeline eligibility behavior itself was not changed.
[p35|Heading 1] 6. Match Lifecycle
[p36|List Bullet] Statuses include Discovered, Accepted, Confirmed, Expired, Declined, and Superseded.
[p37|List Bullet] Close/archive causes Discovered and Accepted matches to expire and leave the active pool; Confirmed remains unchanged.
[p38|List Bullet] Expired matches are hidden from the default active list but can be explicitly viewed with status filters.
[p39|List Bullet] Negotiation is a separate lifecycle and currently starts from Confirmed. Accepted represents partial consent; Confirmed represents quorum/terminal confirmation.
[p40|List Bullet] Republishing an expired opportunity creates a new match record rather than reactivating the old one.
[p41|Heading 1] 7. Automatic Re-evaluation
[p42|List Bullet] Published matching-related opportunity edits now trigger the existing post-publish matching pipeline when the opportunity remains pool-eligible and the matching fingerprint changes.
[p43|List Bullet] Flow: read before snapshot → persist update → check pool eligibility and fingerprint → run existing post-publish matching.
[p44|List Bullet] Fingerprint canonicalizes unordered collections so reordering skills/services/etc. is not treated as a change.
[p45|List Bullet] Title/description and other informational/non-matching fields are excluded from the fingerprint.
[p46|List Bullet] Lifecycle behavior for Accepted/Confirmed/Negotiation was intentionally left unchanged pending business/product decisions.
[p47|Heading 1] 8. Company Matching and Access
[p48|List Bullet] A company matching persistence issue was caused by participant identity: demo/company sessions can use company ID as session.userId while matching persistence requires a human participant.
[p49|List Bullet] A centralized human participant resolution was added for matching persistence.
[p50|List Bullet] Company-to-company, mixed, Two-Way, and Circular matching were covered by tests.
[p51|List Bullet] A later company-access issue was traced to seed data using actingForPartyId for the company party while userId represented the employee. Access/display logic now recognizes actingForPartyId.
